Pacemaker Theory

Core concepts from Ex. No. 2 — Study of Working Principle of Pacemaker

What is a Pacemaker?
An electronic device with a battery, circuits, and memory that generates pacing pulses carried via insulated lead wires to the heart — making the muscle beat at a normal rhythm.

Output: 5V pulse / 10ms
Primary Indication
Bradycardia — an abnormally slow heart rate — is the most frequent reason for pacemaker implantation. The device detects slow rates and compensates with precisely timed electrical stimulation.

Threshold < 60 BPM
Demand Pacemaker
Monitors ECG activity. When the rate drops or a beat is missed, it delivers a pulse. The escape interval equals the time between the last intrinsic beat and the paced beat.

Most Common Type
Fixed-Rate
Discharges at a preset rate constantly, regardless of the heart's own electrical activity. Used when the natural rhythm is completely absent or unreliable.
Rate-Responsive
Has activity sensors that automatically raise or lower the pacing rate to meet the body's metabolic demands during exercise or rest.
Clinical Applications
• Sick Sinus Syndrome
• Atrioventricular (AV) Block
• Bradycardia & Asystole
• Congestive Heart Failure (CRT)
Demand Pacemaker — Block Diagram
QRS Filter Specs
Bandpass: 22–45 Hz · Gain ×10
Input: 1V peak-to-peak
Output: 5V · Range: 15–30 Hz
Selectively isolates QRS complex from noise.
Refractory Generator
Non-retriggerable monostable multivibrator. Generates 250 ms "blind" period after each output pulse or sensed R-wave — prevents sensing T-waves as new beats.
Paced Output
5V amplitude · Duration set by pulse width control (0.1–2.1 ms). Delivered to heart via lead wire. Also fed back to ECG amplifier as reference signal.
